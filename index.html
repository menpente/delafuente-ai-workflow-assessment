Evidence, not magic

AI can create value. It can also destroy it.

Productivity gains depend heavily on the task, the worker, the implementation and the quality of the human-AI workflow. That is why this calculator lets you change every assumption rather than hard-coding one universal “AI uplift”.

+14% average productivity improvement in a study of 5,179 customer-support agents using a generative-AI assistant.
>25% faster completion in an experiment with 758 BCG consultants on tasks that were inside the model’s capability frontier.
−23% performance on a task outside that frontier in the same BCG research — a reminder that “use AI everywhere” is not a strategy.
The point is not to automate the maximum amount of work. It is to identify a workflow where AI improves speed, quality or capacity without weakening judgment, control, privacy or reliability.

How the estimate works

Transparent assumptions. No “AI saves 40%” hand-waving.

LLM is not a synonym for automation. If a process is deterministic, use deterministic technology. A sensible architecture might be n8n or code for orchestration, ordinary APIs and rules for predictable steps, and an LLM only for the parts that require semantic interpretation. The goal is business value, not maximum AI usage.

01

Size the workflow

We calculate the annual labour value currently tied up in the target workflow.

02

Apply only the addressable gain

The benefit is limited by the share of work suitable for AI, expected time reduction and realistic adoption.

03

Subtract the real cost

Implementation and recurring software costs are deducted to show year-one net value and payback.

Formula: annual released hours = people × hours/week × 52 × AI-suitable share × time reduction × adoption. Gross capacity value = released hours × loaded hourly cost. Year-one net value = gross capacity value − implementation cost − annual software cost. ROI = net value ÷ year-one investment. This is a scenario model, not a financial forecast.
